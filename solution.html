Introducing VVU WaterWatch: A Smarter Way to Conserve Water.

A campus process that connects the people who notice water problems with the people who fix them, and measures what changes.

One process, five connected features

Today a leak may go unreported, be reported informally, or be fixed without anyone recording what it cost or saved. WaterWatch gives every step an owner and a record.

Water Monitoring

Authorised administrators record location, date, consumption, unit, meter reading and measurement method.

Water Issue Tracking

Every report moves from Submitted to Under Review, Assigned, In Progress, Resolved and Closed.

Conservation Actions

Community Participation

Impact measurement

Baseline and pilot readings are compared like-for-like to estimate water and cost savings.

Report water wastage

Seen water leaking, a broken pipe, an overflowing tank, a faulty tap or a toilet that keeps flushing? Tell us where and what. It takes about a minute.

Prototype notice: reports are saved in this browser on this device only and are not sent to a central server or to Estates/Facilities. For anything urgent, contact Estates/Facilities directly.

  • Anonymous by default. Add your name only if you want a follow-up.
  • You'll get a reference number to quote when following up.
  • Photos are optional and location metadata is stripped before upload.
  • Report descriptions are not shown on the public pages.

For emergencies such as flooding or burst mains, also contact Estates and Facilities directly.

Severity *
Critical = flooding, burst pipe or continuous heavy flow.
Optional, a clear photo helps Estates locate the problem. Location data in the photo is removed before upload.

If you share contact details they are used only to follow up this report (Data Protection Act, 2012, Act 843).

Applying TRIZ to a real contradiction

How the team resolved the tension between saving water and keeping campus life comfortable.

The contradiction

We want to reduce water consumption and loss (improving parameter) without reducing comfort and convenience for students and staff (worsening parameter). Rationing or restricting supply would save water but make campus life harder, the classic technical contradiction TRIZ is designed to resolve.

The aim is to remove waste, not use: water nobody benefits from, leaks, overflows, taps left running, can be eliminated without anyone feeling a loss of comfort.

Principle 23

Feedback

Introduce a feedback loop: reports, reference numbers, status updates and before/after readings show users that reporting leads to action, which encourages more reporting.

Principle 25

Self service

Let the system's own users detect and report problems, instead of relying only on periodic inspections by a small maintenance team.

Principle 10

Preliminary action

Act before waste occurs: float valves, self-closing nozzles and signage are put in place in advance, so saving water needs no extra effort at the tap.

SCAMPER analysis

SSubstitute

Replace informal word-of-mouth reporting with a structured digital report and reference number.

CCombine

Combine reporting, repair tracking and consumption monitoring in one record, so each repair can be linked to its effect.

AAdapt

Adapt the helpdesk ticket model used in IT support to campus water maintenance.

MModify

Modify the maintenance routine to prioritise by severity and hotspot frequency instead of first come, first served.

PPut to another use

Use existing student phones and the SRC's channels as sensors and awareness tools, no new hardware needed to start.

EEliminate

Eliminate duplicate or lost reports and unrecorded repairs; eliminate guesswork about whether interventions worked.

RReverse or Rearrange

Reverse the flow: instead of facilities searching for leaks, leaks are brought to facilities by the community.

A process innovation

WaterWatch changes how the university finds, fixes and measures water problems. It does not introduce a new product or water technology; it redesigns an existing operational process.

Incremental and sustaining

Incremental: it improves the existing maintenance and reporting process step by step rather than replacing the water system. Sustaining: it strengthens how Estates/Facilities already works and serves the same users better, rather than creating a new market or displacing current providers.

Practical water conservation recommendations

Close taps fully

Turn taps off while brushing or soaping, and check they stop completely when you leave.

Report, don't ignore

A drip you report today is a repair Estates can plan, not a flood they have to react to.

Use only what you need

Fill a bucket instead of running a hose; run washing only with full loads.

Encourage others

Remind roommates and colleagues, social norms change habits faster than signs.
