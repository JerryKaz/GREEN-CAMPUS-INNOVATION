Implementation strategy

Turning Water Conservation into a Campus Wide Practice.

A phased, low cost plan with clear owners, transparent costs and honest measurement.

Six phases from research to adoption

Each phase produces evidence the next one depends on. We only scale what the pilot shows actually works.

  1. 1

    Research and baseline measurement

    Observe, interview and record at least three days of baseline readings.

  2. 2

    Problem analysis and ideation

    Identify root causes; apply TRIZ and SCAMPER to generate solutions.

  3. 3

    WaterWatch prototype development

    Build the reporting, tracking and monitoring prototype.

  4. 4

    Pilot implementation

    Run WaterWatch at selected locations with real users and repairs.

  5. 5

    Measurement and evaluation

    Compare pilot readings with baseline using the same locations and methods.

  6. 6

    Improvement and wider adoption

    Refine the process from feedback and recommend campus-wide rollout.

Water savings calculator

Compare baseline and pilot consumption for the same location and measurement method.

  • Water saved equals baseline minus pilot
  • Percentage reduction equals water saved divided by baseline, times 100 (not calculated when baseline is zero)
  • Financial savings equal water saved times a verified tariff
  • Annual projection only for periods of 7 days or more

All results are estimates, not guaranteed savings.

Students and staff

  • Report leaks
  • Participate in conservation activities

Project team

  • Develop the prototype
  • Document research
  • Analyse results
  • Present recommendations

Anticipating resistance, and responding to it

Type of resistanceWhat it looks likeOur response
CognitiveUsers may not understand why conservation is necessary, water appears plentiful and free to them.Awareness & clear communication: share baseline evidence, costs and the hotspot map in simple language through SRC channels and signage.
EmotionalUsers may resist perceived inconvenience or feel blamed for waste.Reassurance & framing: emphasise that we remove waste, not comfort; anonymous reporting avoids blame; involve users in designing the process.
BehaviouralUsers may continue wasteful habits even when they agree with the goal.Training, feedback & recognition: short sensitisation sessions, visible status updates on reports, pledges, and recognition of active reporters and halls.
